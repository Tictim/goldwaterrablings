Hi. I'm <b>Tictim</b>. I wrote this. I call myself a practitioner of structural art analysis.

Assume the existence of wizards. It is a well-established fact that, to see remote places, wizards use crystalline spheres. The crystal balls may be considered analogous to modern day monitors. And, to project an image into spherical display, the image itself needs to be spherical; thus we can safely assume that crystal balls not merely are display devices, but can also be cameras, inscribing a radial view of its surroundings.

Assume the existence of healthcare. Assume the existence of colonoscopy. If a wizard would conduct a colonoscopy, he needs to insert the camera-ball to the patient, and use a display-ball to observe the interior. But this approach is made impractical by the fact that, once it enters the patient, the position of camera-ball would become hard to control freely. A better approach would be to use multiple equidistant camera-balls, forming a long, straight but flexible, beads. Wizard colonoscopy beads. Suppose, you are the patient, and the wizard-colonoscopist be also a clown. Would you prefer the colonoscopy beads be pre-, or post-juggled?

Coda is a musical passage that brings a music to an end. Multi-movement pieces like sonatas and symphonies may have separate codas for each movement. A coda may be brief or grandiose, dead simple or elaborated, or, not be at all. As a coda is not an independent section, it commonly incorporates materials previously used in the piece, creating a sense of "unified finality".

I have a chronic fascination for a certain species of codas, what I mockingly call "clownified" codas: Where, by pursuit of an intense, explosive passage, the materials get warped and distorted, losing all its original traits; it melds into one carnival-like, festive passage. Treat yourself with [Polovtsian Dances](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5yIiHzMN1kQ). Pay close attention to the individual motives. Then see how the coda turns everything into one giant circus. I'm not saying it's a bad thing, the coda does make sense. I like it and I like the music. But it becomes very weird if I work backwards, isolate the coda, try to see the elements... it rejects me, loses coherence, breaks apart, like as if the colonoscopy beads are all disconnected inside me and I now have to push it all out. Or worse, broke into pieces. AAHHHHH!!!!!

A structural approach on art analysis may be described as such: The art is broken apart into atomic components; the components are each analyzed separately; the analyses are put back in the original order, forming one, coherent analysis. This kind of divide-and-conquer mode of analysis is the backbone of all art theories. But these are all built on a very flimsy assumption. An assumption that an art can be broken down, in a way that the summation represents the original art in its entirety. An assumption that, an art is not a <b>Gestalt</b>.

A Gestalt is an entity. It may be composed of elements. But those elements cannot be dissected, individually understood, then simply put back into Gestalt-1. Gestalt theorists argue that human mind is a Gestalt; however hard you try, to divide the psyche, conquer the neurochemistry, you may never re-create human; because Gestalt is no mere hodge-podge of gears and shafts, it is the cohesion, harmony, it is the supramaximal sum, the unity—however hard you try, you may never sever me, you may never dissect me apart, label my insides, undo my existence into words, never, never, never...

I call myself a practitioner of structural art analysis. Specifically, I analyze video games. Which is such a painful statement to make because I know that video games are Gestalts, that it is a futile effort, that the best I can do is probably a few steps ahead but nothing more than "Don't be like the "Story+gameplay were 2/10 but art and music were 9/10 so overall 7/10" guy". But I insist my greasy hands upon the games because I want to know more. I want to know the language of video games. I want to know how it would look like if the artistry of game-making would, someday, spread its wings fully. And the only thing I can do for, is to beg the Goddess of all Arts an epiphany and toil around. If she slaps me to the ground and kicks me out then that's okay too. At least I can tell you all how I turned myself a clown for the Goddess herself then got slapped to the ground promptly. That should entertain at least somebody.
